How Often Should an Office Be Professionally Cleaned?

July 2026 · 6 min read · The Clean Standard

If you manage an office, you have probably asked some version of this question: how often should an office be professionally cleaned? The honest answer is that it depends on how many people move through the space, what kind of work happens there, and the impression you need the space to make. A five-person accounting office in Westshore and a busy medical practice in St. Pete have very different needs, and cleaning them on the same schedule would leave one over-served and the other falling behind.

This guide breaks down realistic cleaning frequencies by office type and traffic level, so you can set a schedule that keeps your space healthy and presentable without paying for visits you do not need.

The short answer

Most professional offices in Tampa Bay area do well with cleaning two to three times per week, with a lighter daily touch-up for high-traffic areas like restrooms and kitchens. Smaller, low-traffic offices can often run on once or twice a week. High-traffic environments, medical and dental practices, and client-facing spaces usually need daily service.

Frequency is not really about square footage. It is about how fast a space gets dirty and how visible that dirt is to the people who matter — your employees, your clients, and, if you manage a building, your tenants.

What actually drives cleaning frequency

Three factors decide how often your office needs professional attention.

Foot traffic. More people means more dust, more restroom use, more crumbs in the break room, and more wear on floors. A reception area that greets fifty visitors a day needs far more attention than a back office two people share.

The type of work. A software company’s office collects dust and coffee cups. A dental office generates biohazard considerations, requires disinfection of every treatment surface, and is held to hygiene standards that a typical office is not. Food-service adjacent spaces, gyms, and childcare centers all sit at the higher-frequency end for the same reason.

Visibility and stakes. If clients walk through your door, the space is doing marketing work whether you intend it to or not. A scuffed lobby, a smudged glass door, or a restroom that has clearly been skipped tells a visitor something about how the business is run. When first impressions carry weight, frequency goes up.

Cleaning frequency by office type

Small professional office (under 3,000 sq ft, low traffic). One to two visits per week is usually enough. Think trash and recycling, restroom sanitizing, kitchen wipe-down, vacuuming, and dusting of common surfaces. Between visits, the space stays presentable on its own.

Mid-size office (3,000–8,000 sq ft, moderate traffic). Two to three visits per week. Restrooms and kitchens are the pressure points here — they need consistent attention, and a written checklist for each visit keeps the crew from cutting corners on the parts nobody sees.

High-traffic or client-facing office. Daily cleaning, often after hours. Reception, conference rooms, restrooms, and entry glass all reset every night so the space opens clean each morning. Anything less and the wear becomes visible by midweek.

Medical, dental, and healthcare offices. Daily service, with disinfection protocols that go well beyond a standard office clean. High-touch surfaces, waiting rooms, and treatment areas require specific products and a documented process. This is not a space to under-clean.

Coworking and shared spaces. Daily, sometimes more than once a day. Shared desks, phone booths, kitchens, and restrooms turn over constantly, and members notice immediately when the space slips.

Deep cleaning: the schedule most offices forget

Recurring cleaning keeps a space maintained, but it does not replace periodic deep cleaning. Even a well-maintained office should schedule a deeper reset quarterly — carpet extraction, hard-floor scrubbing, high dusting, vents, baseboards, and the spots a routine visit does not reach. Offices with heavy traffic or food areas may want this more often.

Skipping deep cleaning is the most common reason a space that “gets cleaned regularly” still looks tired. The daily and weekly work holds the line; the quarterly work resets it.

A simple way to decide

If you are not sure where your office falls, walk it on a Thursday afternoon — near the end of the week, before the next cleaning. If the restrooms, kitchen, and entry still look presentable, your current frequency is working. If they are visibly worn by midweek, you need more frequent service or a more thorough scope per visit.

Better yet, have a cleaning company assess it in person. A walkthrough takes fifteen minutes and gives you a schedule built around your actual space rather than a generic package.
